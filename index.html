I am currently working at Inria Paris and a member of the MiMove team.

My research interests include machine learning, large-scale peer-to-peer networks, and robust, high-performance distributed systems.

My current work focuses on robust, large-scale machine learning under resource volatility within the Cupseli project, a joint research initiative between Inria and Hivenet. The project explores globally distributed, heterogeneous, and highly volatile computing resources, where nodes continuously join and leave the network.

Although I already hold a PhD in Computer Science, my current research will lead to a second PhD in Computer Science from Sorbonne University.

I previously earned my first PhD in Computer Science from the University of Paris (now Université Paris Cité) where I designed a scalable, secure, fault-tolerant distributed NoSQL database architecture for transactional workloads in very large-scale peer-to-peer networks.

I also hold a Master’s degree in Computer Science from Sorbonne University, with a specialization in Computer Networks, Telecommunications, and Information Security. The program was co-accredited with Télécom Paris.

I also hold a University Research Diploma from Sorbonne University focused on Bitcoin security, particularly against malicious mining strategies such as selfish mining.

  • CP4SC (Cloud Platform for Smart Cities)
    • Collaborators: Inria, Atos, Eviden, Ericsson, Vertical M2M, IFP Energies Nouvelles
    • Responsibilities:
      • Contribution to the CP4SC French research project:
        • Cloud Platform for Smart Cities, a French collaborative project on cloud platforms for smart cities.
      • Occupancy Prediction Prototype Using Federated Learning:
        • Developed a prototype to predict occupancy in BTIC (Atos Business Technology & Innovation Center) building zones from device connection logs, aggregated every 15 minutes, using federated learning. Models were trained locally on each site, and only model updates were shared.
      • Construction of a Multi-Year Dataset (5 Years):
        • Built a consistent 5-year dataset from 511 observed days, using a conservative extension that preserves each zone’s typical hourly and seasonal occupancy profiles.
      • Experimental Validation:
        • Conducted experimental validation on 8 zones. The system preserves daily and monthly occupancy profiles during the test phase and in long-range inference, and clearly outperforms a simplified baseline model.
      • Transferability and Reuse:
        • Released scripts and datasets for reuse in similar research projects.
      • Scientific Publication (In Preparation):
        • Prepared a research paper describing the methodology, dataset construction, and experimental results. The manuscript is ready for submission to an international peer-reviewed conference: “Federated Learning for Occupancy Prediction with Highly Imbalanced, Non-IID, Low-Volume Data: An Empirical Study”.
